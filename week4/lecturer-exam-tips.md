## Kantian ethics: the three requirements
Good intention, universalizability, and respect for persons must all be satisfied.
Having only one (e.g., good intentions) is not enough to make an act ethical — but it does make it "not the worst of the worst."

## Levelling up your answers

Rather than giving a binary "ethical / unethical" verdict, you can qualify the degree — e.g., "unethical, but only to a small extent, because only one of the three criteria failed."
This applies when, for example, intention is ambiguous but the act is otherwise universalizable and respectful.

## CSR example

A company doing CSR for fame lacks good intention, but if the act is universalizable and respectful, the conclusion is "unethical" — yet not totally unethical.
The advice was: flag the nuance rather than just saying "yes/no unethical."

## General exam takeaway

When applying content (Kantian) ethics, analyse all three categorical imperatives separately — failing just one is still a failure, but the severity of the ethical breach depends on how many criteria are unmet.
